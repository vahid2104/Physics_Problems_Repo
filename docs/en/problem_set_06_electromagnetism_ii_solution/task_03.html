Problem 3 – Magnetic Moment of a Loop

Interactive explanation of a current loop in a magnetic field: magnetic moment, torque, maximum torque, potential energy, stable position, and unstable position.

Given Data Magnetic Moment Torque Maximum Torque Potential Energy Stable / Unstable Final Answers

Given Data

We have a conducting loop placed in a uniform magnetic field. The loop has current flowing through it.

Number of turns: \(N\)
Area of loop: \(S\)
Current: \(I\)
Magnetic field: \(\vec B\)

A current loop behaves like a small magnet. The magnetic field tries to rotate the loop until it reaches a stable position.

1. Magnetic Moment of the Loop

The magnetic moment describes how strong the current loop behaves like a magnet.

\[ \vec \mu = NI\vec S \]

The magnitude is:

\[ \mu = NIS \]

Here:

  • \(N\) = number of turns
  • \(I\) = current
  • \(S\) = area of the loop
  • \(\vec S\) = area vector perpendicular to the loop surface

Direction of \(\vec \mu\)

The direction of \(\vec \mu\) is perpendicular to the plane of the loop. We find it using the right-hand rule.

If fingers follow current direction, thumb shows direction of \(\vec \mu\).

2. Torque Acting on the Loop

In a magnetic field, the magnetic moment experiences torque. Torque tries to rotate the loop.

\[ \vec M = \vec \mu \times \vec B \]

The magnitude of the torque is:

\[ M = \mu B \sin\theta \]

Since:

\[ \mu = NIS \]

then:

\[ M = NISB\sin\theta \]

Here \(\theta\) is the angle between \(\vec \mu\) and \(\vec B\).

3. Angle for Maximum Torque

The torque is:

\[ M = NISB\sin\theta \]

The maximum value of \(\sin\theta\) is:

\[ \sin 90^\circ = 1 \]

Therefore, torque is maximum when:

\[ \theta = 90^\circ \]

So the magnetic moment must be perpendicular to the magnetic field:

\[ \vec \mu \perp \vec B \]

The maximum torque is:

\[ M_{\max}=NISB \]

4. Potential Energy

The potential energy of a magnetic moment in a magnetic field is:

\[ U = -\vec \mu \cdot \vec B \]

Using the dot product:

\[ \vec \mu \cdot \vec B = \mu B\cos\theta \]

Therefore:

\[ U = -\mu B\cos\theta \]

Since \(\mu=NIS\), we get:

\[ U = -NISB\cos\theta \]

The loop prefers the position with the lowest potential energy.

5. Stable and Unstable Positions

Stable position means minimum potential energy. Unstable position means maximum potential energy.

Stable Position

\(\theta = 0^\circ\)

\(\vec \mu\) parallel to \(\vec B\)

\(U_{\min}=-NISB\)

Maximum Torque

\(\theta = 90^\circ\)

\(\vec \mu\) perpendicular to \(\vec B\)

\(M_{\max}=NISB\)

Unstable Position

\(\theta = 180^\circ\)

\(\vec \mu\) opposite to \(\vec B\)

\(U_{\max}=+NISB\)

Angle Torque Energy Position
\(0^\circ\) \(0\) \(-NISB\) Stable
\(90^\circ\) \(NISB\) \(0\) Maximum torque
\(180^\circ\) \(0\) \(+NISB\) Unstable

Final Answers

\[ \boxed{\vec \mu = NI\vec S} \]
\[ \boxed{\mu=NIS} \]
\[ \boxed{\vec M=\vec \mu \times \vec B} \]
\[ \boxed{M=NISB\sin\theta} \]
\[ \boxed{M_{\max}=NISB,\quad \theta=90^\circ} \]
\[ \boxed{U=-\vec \mu\cdot\vec B} \]
\[ \boxed{U=-NISB\cos\theta} \]
Stable: \[ \boxed{\theta=0^\circ,\quad U_{\min}=-NISB} \]
Unstable: \[ \boxed{\theta=180^\circ,\quad U_{\max}=+NISB} \]
