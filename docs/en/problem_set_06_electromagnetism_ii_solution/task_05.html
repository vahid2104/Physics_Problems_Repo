Step-by-step Solution

1. Determine the induced EMF ε

When a conducting rod moves in a magnetic field, the free charges inside the rod move together with the rod. Because moving charges are inside a magnetic field, they feel magnetic force.

F = q(v × B)

This force separates charges. One end becomes positive and the other end becomes negative. This creates an induced EMF.

The formula for motional EMF is:

ε = B L v sinθ

Here the motion is perpendicular to the magnetic field:

θ = 90°,   sin90° = 1

So:

ε = B L v

Substitute the values:

ε = (0.6)(0.25)(4)
ε = 0.6 V
ε = 0.6 V

2. Determine the potential difference between the ends

In this case, the potential difference between the ends of the rod is equal to the induced EMF.

ΔV = ε

Since:

ε = 0.6 V

Then:

ΔV = 0.6 V

So the two ends of the rod have a potential difference of 0.6 volts.

3. What happens if the motion is not perpendicular to B?

If the rod does not move perpendicular to the magnetic field, then we use the general formula:

ε = B L v sinθ

Only the perpendicular component of velocity produces EMF.

v⊥ = v sinθ

If θ becomes smaller, sinθ becomes smaller, so the EMF also becomes smaller.

If the rod moves parallel to the magnetic field:

θ = 0°,   sin0° = 0

Then:

ε = 0

So, motion parallel to the magnetic field produces no induced EMF.

4. How does ε depend on L?

The formula is:

ε = B L v

If B and v are constant, then EMF depends directly on the length of the rod.

ε ∝ L

This means: if the rod becomes longer, the EMF becomes larger.

For example, if L is doubled:

Lnew = 2L
εnew = B(2L)v = 2BLv
If L doubles, ε also doubles.

5. Where does the energy of the electric field come from?

The energy does not come from the magnetic field itself.

The magnetic field helps separate the charges, but the magnetic force does no work because it is perpendicular to the motion of charges.

The energy comes from the external mechanical work needed to keep the rod moving.

Mechanical energy → Electrical energy
The energy comes from the external force moving the rod.

So, when we push or pull the rod through the magnetic field, our mechanical work is converted into electrical energy.
