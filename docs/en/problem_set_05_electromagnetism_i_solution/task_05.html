Problem 5 – Capacitor: Energy and Force

Step-by-step explanation with animated parallel plate capacitor

Given Data

We have a parallel plate capacitor.

Plate area: S = 0.02 m²
Distance between plates: d = 5 mm = 0.005 m
Voltage: U = 500 V
Permittivity of free space: ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m
We assume the space between the plates is air or vacuum. Therefore, we use ε = ε₀.

When voltage increases, the electric field becomes stronger. When the plate distance becomes smaller, the electric field and force become larger.

1. Calculate the Capacitance

For a parallel plate capacitor, the capacitance is:

C = ε₀S / d

Substitute the values:

C = (8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.02) / 0.005

First calculate:

0.02 / 0.005 = 4

So:

C = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 4
C = 35.4 × 10⁻¹² F
C = 3.54 × 10⁻¹¹ F = 35.4 pF

2. Calculate the Energy Stored

The energy stored in a capacitor is:

W = ½CU²

Substitute the values:

W = ½ × 3.54 × 10⁻¹¹ × 500²

Calculate the square:

500² = 250000

Now:

W = ½ × 3.54 × 10⁻¹¹ × 250000
W = 4.43 × 10⁻⁶ J
W = 4.43 × 10⁻⁶ J = 4.43 μJ

3. Calculate the Electric Field Intensity

Between two parallel plates, the electric field is approximately uniform.

E = U / d

Substitute the values:

E = 500 / 0.005
E = 100000 V/m
E = 1.00 × 10⁵ V/m

The electric field points from the positive plate to the negative plate.

4. Calculate the Field Energy Density

The energy density of an electric field is:

w = ½ε₀E²

Substitute the values:

w = ½ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × (1.00 × 10⁵)²

First calculate:

(1.00 × 10⁵)² = 1.00 × 10¹⁰

So:

w = ½ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 1.00 × 10¹⁰
w = 0.04425 J/m³
w ≈ 0.0443 J/m³

5. Calculate the Force of Attraction

The electric field creates pressure on the plates. This pressure is equal to the field energy density:

p = w = ½ε₀E²

Force is pressure multiplied by area:

F = pS

Since p = w:

F = wS

Substitute the values:

F = 0.04425 × 0.02
F = 0.000885 N
F = 8.85 × 10⁻⁴ N

This is a small attraction force between the two capacitor plates.
