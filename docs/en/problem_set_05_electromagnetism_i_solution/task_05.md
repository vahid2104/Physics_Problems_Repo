# Problem 5 – Capacitor: energy and force

We have a parallel plate capacitor with:

$$
S=0.02\ \mathrm{m^2}
$$

$$
d=5\ \mathrm{mm}
$$

$$
U=500\ \mathrm{V}
$$

We need to calculate:

* the capacitance,
* the energy stored in the capacitor,
* the electric field intensity,
* the field energy density,
* the force of attraction between the plates.

---

## Given data

The plate area is

$$
S=0.02\ \mathrm{m^2}
$$

The distance between the plates is

$$
d=5\ \mathrm{mm}
$$

Since

$$
1\ \mathrm{mm}=10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}
$$

we write

$$
d=5\times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{m}
$$

So

$$
d=0.005\ \mathrm{m}
$$

The voltage is

$$
U=500\ \mathrm{V}
$$

The permittivity of free space is

$$
\varepsilon_0=8.85\times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}
$$

We assume the capacitor has air or vacuum between the plates, so

$$
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_0
$$

---

## 1. Capacitance of the capacitor

For a parallel plate capacitor, the capacitance is

$$
C=\frac{\varepsilon_0 S}{d}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
C=\frac{(8.85\times 10^{-12})(0.02)}{0.005}
$$

First calculate the fraction:

$$
\frac{0.02}{0.005}=4
$$

So

$$
C=(8.85\times 10^{-12})(4)
$$

$$
C=35.4\times 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

This can also be written as

$$
C=3.54\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

Therefore,

$$
C\approx 3.54\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

or

$$
C\approx 35.4\ \mathrm{pF}
$$

because

$$
1\ \mathrm{pF}=10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

---

## 2. Energy stored in the capacitor

The energy stored in a capacitor is

$$
W=\frac{1}{2}CU^2
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
W=\frac{1}{2}(3.54\times 10^{-11})(500)^2
$$

First calculate

$$
500^2=250000
$$

So

$$
W=\frac{1}{2}(3.54\times 10^{-11})(250000)
$$

Multiply:

$$
(3.54\times 10^{-11})(250000)=8.85\times 10^{-6}
$$

Now multiply by

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
W=4.43\times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

Therefore,

$$
W\approx 4.43\times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

or

$$
W\approx 4.43\ \mu\mathrm{J}
$$

---

## 3. Electric field intensity between the plates

For a parallel plate capacitor, the electric field is approximately uniform.

The electric field intensity is

$$
E=\frac{U}{d}
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
E=\frac{500}{0.005}
$$

Since

$$
0.005=5\times 10^{-3}
$$

we get

$$
E=100000\ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

Therefore,

$$
E=1.00\times 10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

The electric field points from the positive plate to the negative plate.

---

## 4. Field energy density

The energy density of an electric field is

$$
w=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
w=\frac{1}{2}(8.85\times 10^{-12})(1.00\times 10^5)^2
$$

First calculate

$$
(1.00\times 10^5)^2=1.00\times 10^{10}
$$

So

$$
w=\frac{1}{2}(8.85\times 10^{-12})(1.00\times 10^{10})
$$

Multiply:

$$
(8.85\times 10^{-12})(1.00\times 10^{10})
$$

$$
=8.85\times 10^{-2}
$$

$$
=0.0885
$$

Now multiply by

$$
\frac{1}{2}
$$

$$
w=0.04425\ \mathrm{J/m^3}
$$

Therefore,

$$
w\approx 4.43\times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{J/m^3}
$$

or

$$
w\approx 0.0443\ \mathrm{J/m^3}
$$

---

## 5. Force of attraction between the plates

The electric field creates pressure on the capacitor plates.

This electric pressure is equal to the field energy density:

$$
p=w
$$

So

$$
p=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_0 E^2
$$

The force is pressure multiplied by area:

$$
F=pS
$$

So

$$
F=wS
$$

Substitute the values:

$$
F=(0.04425)(0.02)
$$

Multiply:

$$
F=0.000885\ \mathrm{N}
$$

Therefore,

$$
F=8.85\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

So the plates attract each other with a force of about

$$
F\approx 8.85\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

This is a small force, less than one thousandth of a newton.

---

## Final answers for Problem 5

The capacitance is

$$
C\approx 3.54\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}
$$

or

$$
C\approx 35.4\ \mathrm{pF}
$$

The energy stored in the capacitor is

$$
W\approx 4.43\times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}
$$

or

$$
W\approx 4.43\ \mu\mathrm{J}
$$

The electric field intensity between the plates is

$$
E=1.00\times 10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}
$$

The field energy density is

$$
w\approx 0.0443\ \mathrm{J/m^3}
$$

The force of attraction between the plates is

$$
F\approx 8.85\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}
$$

So the final result is:

$$
\boxed{C\approx 3.54\times 10^{-11}\ \mathrm{F}}
$$

$$
\boxed{W\approx 4.43\times 10^{-6}\ \mathrm{J}}
$$

$$
\boxed{E=1.00\times 10^5\ \mathrm{V/m}}
$$

$$
\boxed{w\approx 0.0443\ \mathrm{J/m^3}}
$$

$$
\boxed{F\approx 8.85\times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{N}}
$$

✅
