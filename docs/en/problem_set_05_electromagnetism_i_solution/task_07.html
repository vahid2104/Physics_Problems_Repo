Problem 7 – Motion in a Central Field

Electric central force, radial motion, RK4 method, energy cases, and gravitational analogy

Given Electric Field

The electric field is

E(r) = k Q / r²   r̂

Here, Q is the source charge placed at the origin. The symbol r̂ means the radial direction.

If the source charge is positive, the electric field points outward. If the source charge is negative, the electric field points inward.

1. Equation of Motion

The electric force on a particle with charge q is

F = qE

Substitute the electric field:

F = k qQ / r²   r̂

Using Newton’s second law:

F = ma

So the equation of motion becomes

m d²r⃗/dt² = k qQ / r²   r̂

Therefore,

d²r⃗/dt² = k qQ / (m r²)   r̂

2. Radial Motion

Radial motion means the particle moves only toward or away from the center. There is no angular motion.

So the vector equation becomes one-dimensional:

d²r/dt² = k qQ / (m r²)

Define

α = k qQ / m

Then

d²r/dt² = α / r²

If α > 0, the force is repulsive. If α < 0, the force is attractive.

3. RK4 Method Setup

RK4 works with first-order equations, so we define velocity:

v = dr/dt

Now the system becomes:

dr/dt = v
dv/dt = α / r²

This is the system solved numerically in the animation below.

Interactive RK4 Animation

Use the controls to change the motion. The particle is simulated using the RK4 method.

Tip: Try opposite signs for Q and q to get attraction. Try same signs for repulsion. Increase the initial velocity to move from negative energy to positive energy.

4. Positive and Negative Energy

The total mechanical energy is

E = 1/2 mv² + k qQ / r

The first term is kinetic energy. The second term is electric potential energy.

Attractive Case

If the charges have opposite signs:

qQ < 0

Then the potential energy is negative:

U(r) = - k |qQ| / r
Energy Meaning
E < 0 Bound motion. The particle does not have enough energy to escape.
E = 0 Escape limit. The particle just escapes with zero final speed.
E > 0 Escape motion. The particle escapes with nonzero final speed.

Repulsive Case

If the charges have the same sign:

qQ > 0

The potential energy is positive. The particle is pushed away from the center.

5. Escape Speed

For attractive motion, escape happens when

E = 0

So

1/2 mv² = k |qQ| / r

Solving for velocity gives

v_escape = √(2k |qQ| / mr)

If the initial velocity is smaller than this value, energy is negative. If it is larger, energy is positive.

6. Gravitational Analogy

The gravitational force is

F_g = -G Mm / r²

The gravitational potential energy is

U_g = -G Mm / r

The electric force is

F_e = k qQ / r²

The electric potential energy is

U_e = k qQ / r
Electric Field Gravitational Field
Can be attractive or repulsive Always attractive
Depends on charges q and Q Depends on masses m and M
Force proportional to 1/r² Force proportional to 1/r²
Potential proportional to 1/r Potential proportional to 1/r

The attractive electric case is mathematically very similar to gravity. The main difference is that electric forces can also be repulsive.

Final Summary

Equation of motion:
d²r⃗/dt² = kqQ / (mr²) r̂
Radial motion:
d²r/dt² = α / r²
RK4 system:
dr/dt = v
dv/dt = α / r²
Energy:
E = 1/2 mv² + kqQ/r

For attractive fields, negative energy means bound motion and positive energy means escape motion. This is the same idea as in gravitational motion.
