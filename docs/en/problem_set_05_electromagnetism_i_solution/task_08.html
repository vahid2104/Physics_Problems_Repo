2. Energy for Equilateral Triangle

In an equilateral triangle, all distances are equal:

r₁₂ = r₁₃ = r₂₃ = a

If all three charges are equal and positive:

q₁ = q₂ = q₃ = q

Then the total energy becomes:

Utotal = 3kq²/a
Substitute:
Utotal = 3(8.99 × 10⁹)(4 × 10⁻⁶)² / 0.3
First:
(4 × 10⁻⁶)² = 16 × 10⁻¹²
Then:
kq² = (8.99 × 10⁹)(16 × 10⁻¹²)
kq² = 0.14384
Now:
Utotal = 3(0.14384 / 0.3)
Utotal = 1.4384 J
Final answer:
Utotal ≈ 1.44 J

3. Changing the Sign of One Charge

Now we make one charge negative:

q₁ = +q,   q₂ = +q,   q₃ = -q

The total energy becomes:

Utotal = kq²/a - kq²/a - kq²/a

So:

Utotal = -kq²/a
Substitute:
Utotal = -(0.14384 / 0.3)
Utotal = -0.47947 J

The energy becomes negative because opposite charges attract.

Energy change:

ΔU = -0.47947 - 1.4384

4. Minimum Energy Configuration Numerically

If point charges are completely free, opposite charges can get closer and closer.

U = -kq²/r

When:

r → 0

then:

U → -∞
So without restrictions, there is no finite minimum energy for opposite charges.

To calculate a numerical minimum, we add restrictions:

Box size = 1 m × 1 m
Minimum distance = 0.05 m

Case A: Three Positive Charges

For three positive charges, all forces are repulsive. The charges want to move far apart.

U = kq²(1/r₁₂ + 1/r₁₃ + 1/r₂₃)

A low-energy configuration is placing charges at three corners of a square:

(0,0), (1,0), (0,1)

The distances are:

r₁₂ = 1,   r₁₃ = 1,   r₂₃ = √2
U = 0.14384(1 + 1 + 1/√2)
Umin ≈ 0.389 J

Case B: Two Positive Charges and One Negative Charge

The negative charge attracts both positive charges.

U = kq²(1/r₁₂ - 1/r₁₃ - 1/r₂₃)

A low-energy restricted configuration is:

r₁₃ = 0.05 m
r₂₃ = 0.05 m
r₁₂ = 0.10 m
U = 0.14384(10 - 20 - 20)
Umin ≈ -4.32 J

5. Stability Interpretation

All Positive Charges

All charges repel each other.

The energy is positive.

The system is not stable if the charges are free to move.

They spread out to reduce the energy.

Mixed Signs

Positive and negative charges attract.

The energy becomes negative.

The system is more bound.

But ideal point charges can collapse together without restrictions.
